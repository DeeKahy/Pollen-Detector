In Denmark the daily pollen counts come from two stations, one in Copenhagen for eastern Denmark and one in Viborg for western
Denmark~\cite{AstmaAllergi}. Pollen levels for other places are estimates based on the nearest
station, so they show a general trend rather than a local measurement
\fillin{source for this, e.g. the Astma-Allergi Danmark page that says so}. Pollen levels change
with local plants, terrain and weather, so a person living far from a station can have quite
different conditions from the published numbers.

\section{How pollen is measured today}

The stations use a volumetric trap of the Hirst type~\cite{Hirst1952,EN16868}. A pump pulls
air through a narrow slit onto a sticky tape on a slowly turning drum. Once a week the tape is
taken out, stained and counted by hand under a microscope by a trained person. The method is
the reference standard and works well, but it has two problems for local information:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cost.} The traps and the trained staff are expensive, so there are only two
  stations. \fillin{Price of a trap, with a source. Burkard does not list prices so ask Florian or something or
  Astma-Allergi Danmark, or find a tender document.}
  \item \textbf{Manual work.} Every count is done by a trained person, which limits how many
  samples can be analysed~\cite{Tummon2024}. \fillin{Florian told us this. Check that
  Tummon et al.\ 2024 says it, or find a better source.}
\end{itemize}

Automated monitors exist, such as the Swisens Poleno Jupiter~\cite{SwisensPoleno}. They identify
pollen without a person, but they are specialised instruments with a price far above what a
dense network can afford. Research prototypes show that cheap optics can work: Wu et
al.~\cite{Wu2018} used a phone-based microscope without a lens and deep learning to recognise
pollen and spores.

\section{Idea: a pollen monitor anyone can build}

The fine dust the problem has already been solved in a different way. Sensor.Community (started
as luftdaten.info in Stuttgart) and they let anyone build a cheap particle sensor from a guide, put it
outside, and share the data on a public map~\cite{SensorCommunity}. Many of these sensors
now give much more local data than the official stations. No similar low-cost, open approach
exists for pollen as far as we can tell, because a particle counter cannot tell pollen types apart.

Our idea is to do the same for pollen. The device should be cheap enough (around 100 to 150
euro) and simple enough that anyone can build one from a guide. Like the reference trap it
catches particles on sticky tape, but instead of a person with a microscope, a camera takes
pictures of the tape and software counts the pollen by type.

We see two kinds of pottential users:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{People and communities} who build a station at home. All stations send their
  counts to a shared server, which can combine them into a local pollen map or forecast, like a
  weather forecast.
  \item \textbf{The people running the current stations}, who could set up some of our stations
  around the country. Each station should hopefully only need a new roll of tape every few weeks or months,
  and a check against a reference trap now and then to stay calibrated.
\end{itemize}

We think all of it should be free: the design, the software and the data. The software is
open source (AGPL-3.0) and the 3D models are public domain (CC0).

This project builds on an earlier open-source pollen detector by

to make the device easier to build, but the electronics and the basic way it works (a fan, a

addition so far is a colour LED for lighting the tape.

\section{Research question}\label{sec:research-question}

We want to find out whether this is possible at all, and how far it can go. Our main question is:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How far can a cheap, open, self-built device go towards automated pollen monitoring
that anyone can build?}
\end{quote}

To answer it we split it into smaller questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Can the device catch pollen-sized particles from the air on tape?
  \item Can a cheap camera without a lens show those particles well enough to recognise them?
  \item What does a station cost, and how much upkeep does it need (tape, cleaning, calibration)?
\end{enumerate}
